# Style archetypes

Four production styles cover every high-performing reference studied (15 shorts, 7 channels).
Choose one per video. Each is a *system*: a character design, a world look, a motion technique,
an edit rhythm, a caption preset and an audio bed that work together. Mixing systems looks amateur;
committing to one looks like a channel.

## A. Toon-3D egg-head explainer
Seen in: process/ladder explainers about swords, a western story about revolvers and a WWI pilot story, from three different small channels.
These three channels share a near-identical kit, which strongly suggests a templated/code-driven 3D pipeline.

**Characters.** Large ovoid "egg" head (~40 % of height) with a thick black outline; a **flat 2D decal face**
(bar brows, dot or slit eyes, one-line mouth; extremes: gritted-teeth grid, `> <` squint, O-mouth). Expressions
**swap on a single frame** — never morph. Small 3D costume body (apron, vest, gi, flight jacket) on thin black
tube limbs. Identity = costume + hat/hair.

**World.** Low-poly toon sets: faceted mountains, cone pines, cotton-ball clouds, flat-shaded wood interiors.
Bloom, depth of field, warm haze. Dusk mauve/amber or sun-bleached cream palettes with one saturated accent
(the red plane, the yellow-hot blade).

**Motion.** True 30 fps keyframed motion; slow camera push / truck / crane on every shot; prop orbits;
rigid-body splits for "cut" moments (tomato halves, straw mat, atom pyramid); X-ray material swap for
mechanisms (object turns to glass, a glowing ring + red arrow on the key part).

**Edit.** The process-explainer channel: 5–7 hard cuts, shots 4–24 s, beats happen *inside* long shots. The western story: whip pans
between speakers (4–6 blurred frames), environment-swap montage cut on 1–2-frame white flashes. The pilot story:
fast aerial grammar, 1.5–3 s shots, "push through the pupil" concept transition, iris-in exits.

**Graphics.** Diegetic: wooden signs that drop in ("10 TONS / IRON SAND"), a 3D scoreboard counting exactly with
the VO, a persistent navy/gold **LEVEL N** badge for escalation ladders, blueprint-grid diagram scenes (royal
blue + white grid, uppercase white/orange labels popping into empty space).

**Hook/end.** Show the end-state transformation in the first 3 s (straight blade into water → curved). End on
the same location/glint so it loops.

## B. AI toon story
Seen in: a large channel with four videos at 7–30M views, plus two small channels (an F1 story at 4.7M views on 1.8k subscribers, and a prison-escape story).

**Characters.** Cyanide-&-Happiness DNA: big round head (white/peach, 35–40 % of height), no neck, dot eyes,
`^ ^` grins, **rectangular grid-of-teeth grimace**, stick limbs with ball hands, capsule torso that *is* the
outfit, thick black outline. Roles coded by **one costume signifier** (engineer cap, bowler + waistcoat, cop cap,
beanie + sunglasses). Protagonist gets a distinctive body colour.

**World.** "2.5D toon-3D": real perspective, global illumination, volumetric god-rays, bloom, shallow DOF, ink
outlines on sets — a cel-shaded game-engine look. (The single flat-2D variant was the lowest performer.)
One small channel pushes to max-saturation primaries + chibi proportions; another renders webcomic characters as glossy
3D dioramas.

**Motion.** AI image-to-video, true 30 fps. Every clip prompted with one slow camera move + one action.
Tells to hide: morphing labels, garbled small text, faces drifting. Keep clips short (keep the best 1.5–4 s)
and let speed hide inconsistency.

**Edit.** ~2 s per shot, **one shot per narration clause**, each a literal sight gag of the words
("nothing" → an empty gift box). The large channel hides cuts with **6–9-frame crossdissolves** and in-camera
**pass-through** moves (through a door, over a cliff, into a phone screen); only 1–7 hard cuts, kept for
punchlines. The prison-escape story does the opposite: ~1 s hard cuts, whip-pan smears, crash zooms, handheld shake on tense
beats, a 0.15–0.5 s syllable-paced montage at the climax.

**Graphics.** Exposition via **big diegetic signage baked into the keyframe** (room signs, banners, newspaper
headlines, tombstones, phone UIs). The F1 story adds a thin sticker layer: colour-coded tilted pill labels
(red = rule/penalty, gold = info, green = good, white = time/place), place badges, onomatopoeia ("glug",
"click!"), ✕/✓ stamps, a blueprint/hologram mode for the explanation.

**Colour as story.** Bright + saturated setup → cold blue / night / rain exactly at the twist or explanation.
Recurring motif: **one warm window in a cold frame** marks the secret. The prison story arcs cold blue (oppression) →
warm amber (kindness).

## C. Rigged 2D comedic explainer
Seen in: one history channel (4 videos, 9–29M views; a credited narrator and two animators).

**Characters.** Chibi humans with **plain white circle heads (~1/3 of height)**, no nose/ears, small black oval
eyes or half-lid arcs, single-stroke brows, white 4-finger glove hands, pear bodies, stubby legs. Identity
comes *only* from lovingly accurate period headgear/uniform (shako, kepi, bearskin, M35 helmet) or
hair/moustache. Emotion via emoji shorthand: sweat drop, tear streams, spiral eyes, "…", smug half-lids.

**Rendering.** Thick dark tapered outline (~5–7 px at 1080 h on silhouettes, 2–3 px inside); one soft
shadow + highlight per material; painted mid-detail backgrounds and maps in the same outline language.
Clean, no grain. Palette follows topic (night blue-grey, field green, London red/cream, parchment sepia).

**Motion.** Cut-out puppet rig at true 30 fps (*not* on twos): eased rotations of rigid parts, constant subtle
idle bob/tilt, **1–2-frame replacement-pose swaps timed to the stressed word**. Only FX are frame-by-frame
(outlined smoke puffs, smear globe, jagged muzzle stars).

**Edit.** New setup on nearly every sentence (≈ 20 setups in 85 s, 3–5 s each) with bursts of 0.3–1.5 s
shots around a joke. At least one beat-synced event per shot (pose swap, prop pop, UI card) on the stressed
word. Slow push/drift on every held shot, 3–8-frame shakes on every impact. One **showpiece transition every
10–15 s**: whip blur, radial zoom-swirl, white flash, full-screen smoke/gas/explosion fill, split screen,
character walking past camera.

**Graphics/gags.** Literal visual puns of the narration; game/meme UI as punchlines (kill-feed, "CRITICAL HIT",
reward popup, ammo counter, QTE ring, stat card); illustrated maps with territory fills fading in on the
spoken name; year counters. (Reference parodies of specific games are a legal grey area — make your own
generic UI instead.)

**Hook/end.** Open on a costumed character mid-action with a second-person question. End on a punchline, then
either a SUBSCRIBE graphic built into the last gag (lettered into smoke, slammed-in bar) or an ironic loop.

## D. Data motion-graphics
Seen in: one small channel's "spend $1 trillion in 24 hours" video.

**Characters.** One bean-shaped rubber-hose mascot (noodle arms) with swappable whole-face drawings
(deadpan default, shock circles, happy arcs, sweat drop), cut on the emotional word.

**World.** Flat 2D vector, muted indigo/purple + gold, pastel day scenes, red danger wash, vignette + edge blur.
A **home-base set** (desk/phone/timer) the video keeps returning to between excursions.

**Motion.** After-Effects-style rig; props slide, swing and topple as rigid layers; slow push-ins and long
pans across one illustration. Low cut rate because the picture changes *inside* shots.

**Graphics (the star).** Persistent counter HUD: dark rounded box, tracked micro-label ("TIME LEFT",
"EVERY SECOND"), big gold digits that roll over 0.5–1.5 s on the spoken number and turn red at danger
thresholds. Swinging price tags, rubber stamps (SOLD / PAID / CANCELED) landing on the word, a "look-here"
ripple ring (80 → 140 %, fades in 0.5 s) around the named object.

**End.** Seamless loop: last clause runs grammatically into the first line over the opening frame.

## Universal constants
True across every archetype — treat these as rules unless you have a reason:
- 9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps; 45–90 s (60 s is the sweet spot for stories).
- **Narration starts at 0.0 s** and covers 94–99 % of the runtime; ends ≤ 1.5 s before the final frame.
- **Nothing is animated on twos.** Everything moves at a smooth 30 fps; no shot is ever fully static
  (slow push, drift, idle bob).
- Captions: centred, vertical centre at **≈ 68–72 % of frame height** (above the Shorts UI, below faces),
  1–3 words per card (up to ~6 when building word by word), white with dark outline and/or shadow.
  Top ~15 % and bottom ~20 % of the frame stay free of important content (platform UI).
- Every concrete noun in the narration gets a visual. A muted viewer should follow the story.
- Mastered to **−14 LUFS integrated**, true peak ≤ −1 dBTP (13 of 15 references land at −14.0 ± 0.7).
- No intros, no logos, no "thanks for watching". The ending is designed to loop.
